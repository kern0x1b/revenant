# CoreImage's features: what the host answers, what the port carries, and what is open

The port carries three feature classes of CoreImage, `CIRectangleFeature` and `CIQRCodeFeature` (`Graphics/CIFeature8.m`,
with `CIQRCodeFeature.symbolDescriptor` in `Graphics/CIQRCodeFeature11.m`) and `CITextFeature` (`Graphics/CIFeature9.m`), and
the three `CIFeatureType*` constants (`Graphics/CIConstants90.m`). The registry rows are
`registry/CoreImage/features8.json`, `features9.json`, `features11.json` and the three rows of `constants10.json`.

Nothing in the port makes a feature by detection. A feature is made by the private initializers the differential calls through
a category of its own, and the detectors (`CIDetector`, `CIDetectorType*` as a detector's type, and its options) are not carried:
that is slice 2, and it is open here.

`tests/backports/host/cifeature/` is the differential. `run.sh` builds `differential.m` twice, against the host's CoreImage and
against the port's sources, and compares the two outputs field by field. Its verdict on the measurement of 2026-10-15:

    EQUAL: 41 lines, the same on both sides once the known lines are apart
    PINNED: the 9 host and 7 port known lines match their pins
    caught: QR type answers the rectangle constant
    caught: text bottom-right corner lost
    caught: fresh QR messageString answers a string (known pin only)
    verdict: 0 difference(s)

The host is the macOS CoreImage of this machine, not the iOS 6.1.3 release: the differential says what the host's CoreImage
answers, and the 6.1.3 release is not run here (see the open items).

## Measured answers (the host's own CoreImage)

- **Detected QR code** of "Hello QR", generated by the host's `CIQRCodeGenerator` at 8x and detected by the host's QR detector:
  `bounds {8 8 168 168}`, corners topLeft (8, 176), topRight (176, 176), bottomLeft (8, 8), bottomRight (176, 8),
  `messageString` "Hello QR", `symbolDescriptor` a `CIQRCodeDescriptor`. Conforms to NSSecureCoding and NSCopying; the copy is
  equal to the original.
- **Detected rectangle** of a filled black 240 by 180 rectangle at (80, 60) in a 400 by 300 bitmap: `bounds {79.34 59.41 240.77 181.02}`,
  corners topLeft (79.34, 240.16), topRight (319.85, 240.42), bottomLeft (79.59, 59.41), bottomRight (320.10, 59.67). Conforms to
  neither protocol; no `messageString`, no `symbolDescriptor`.
- **Detected text** of "HELLO WORLD" in Helvetica-Bold 48 point, with sub-features asked for: `bounds {23 39 352 37}`, corners
  topLeft (23, 76), topRight (375, 76), bottomLeft (23, 39), bottomRight (375, 39), and zero sub-features on this image.
  Conforms to neither protocol. The host's `CITextFeature` answers `messageString` with nil, a selector the public header
  does not declare on that class (it is declared on `CIQRCodeFeature` only, CIFeature.h).
- **Superclass** of all three: `CIFeature`. **Types**: `QRCode`, `Rectangle`, `Text`, as the constants `CIFeatureTypeQRCode`,
  `CIFeatureTypeRectangle`, `CIFeatureTypeText` print.
- **QR round trip**: the host's keyed-coding round trip does not return the feature it was given. The archive stores the geometry
  as one scalar per key (`bounds.suffix` 168, `topLeft.suffix` 8, `topRight.suffix` 176, `bottomLeft.suffix` 8,
  `bottomRight.suffix` 176, read with `plutil -p` from the host's archive), and decodes to `bounds {168 168 168 168}` and
  corners TL (8, 8), TR (176, 176), BL (8, 8), BR (176, 176). The decode rule that produces this is not measured, and the port
  does not copy it. The port's round trip is the identity: `equal 1`.
- **Fresh QR feature** (`[[CIQRCodeFeature alloc] init]`): zero bounds and corners; `messageString` raises
  NSInvalidArgumentException on the host; `symbolDescriptor` answers nil.

## Measured answers (the iOS 6.1.3 release, emulated)

`tests/backports/emulate/ciqr-detector/` is the probe: it calls the release's own `+[CIDetector detectorOfType:context:options:]`
with `nil` context and `nil` options, once per type, and prints the class answered, `nil`, or the exception raised. The types
are the strings the host's constants hold (`CIDetectorTypeQRCode` and `CIDetectorTypeRectangle` in `Graphics/CIConstants80.m`,
`CIDetectorTypeText` in `Graphics/CIConstants90.m`), named directly, so the probe does not depend on whether the release exports
the constants (that export is not measured here; see the open items). The run:
`xmake emulate -d iPhone4,1 -r 6.1.3 run /usr/libexec/ciqrdetector` on the emulator's 6.1.3 (10B329) image, measured 2026-10-15.

    ciqr-detector: CIDetectorTypeFace -> CIFaceCoreDetector     (control: the release's face detector)
    ciqr-detector: CIDetectorTypeQRCode -> nil
    ciqr-detector: CIDetectorTypeRectangle -> nil
    ciqr-detector: CIDetectorTypeText -> nil

So the release answers nil for the three types, and an application that asks for one gets no detector at all. No exception is raised for any of the three. The face answer is the release's own and is not changed by the port. This
is one run of one probe on the emulator, not a device run.

## The four known differences (pinned, not compared)

`run.sh` compares every line that is not a known difference as a plain equality. The four known differences are the lines
that start with `known: ` in `differential.m`'s output. Their exact host and port lines are recorded in
`tests/backports/host/cifeature/known-host.txt` and `known-port.txt`, and the run is red if either side changes, so each pin is
checked on every run and not remembered.

1. **`symbolDescriptor`** of a detected QR feature: the host answers a `CIQRCodeDescriptor`, the port answers nil. The port makes
   no QR feature with a descriptor: the descriptor is the symbol's codewords after error correction, and the decoder that would
   read them is not carried. A fresh feature answers nil on both sides.
2. **QR round trip**: the port's round trip is the identity (`equal 1`), the host's loses the geometry (`equal 0`, the measured
   bounds and corners above). The port keeps its lossless layout, as the header's NSSecureCoding promises. The host's defect is
   not copied.
3. **Fresh QR `messageString`**: the host raises NSInvalidArgumentException (its message is read from a descriptor a fresh feature
   does not have); the port answers nil, which the header allows.
4. **Text `messageString`**: the host answers nil on `CITextFeature`, for the selector the public header does not declare on that
   class. The port does not answer it, and does not copy an undeclared selector. The differential probes the selector on both
   sides, so this difference is pinned rather than dropped from the probe.

## Open items

- **The detector** (slice 2, not this slice): `CIDetector` and the detector options (`CIDetectorReturnSubFeatures` is a constant
  already carried) are not carried. Until it is, a feature comes only from the initializers, and no application gets a
  `CIQRCodeFeature` or `CIRectangleFeature` from a detector on the port.
- **`symbolDescriptor` on the port**: nil for every feature it makes (known difference 1). The registry row is `implemented` with
  that answer, which the header allows; whether the gate accepts that status for a nil answer is the gate's decision and is
  reported with the gate verdict.
- **The iOS 6.1.3 release**: whether the release exports the three `CIFeatureType*` symbols, and whether it carries the three
  classes, is not measured here. The constants rows say so in their `reason`.
- **The `introduced` of the constants** is the gate's measurement on the release ladder: `CIFeatureTypeQRCode` and
  `CIFeatureTypeRectangle` first appear in iOS 9.0, and `CIFeatureTypeText` in 9.0, so all three sit in `Graphics/CIConstants90.m`
  and their rows say 9.0. The first attempt put the QR and rectangle pair in the 8.0 file, and the gate refused it
  ("CIConstants80.m defines ... CIFeatureTypeQRCode CIFeatureTypeRectangle from iOS 9.0"). The classes are 8.0 from the header
  (`NS_CLASS_AVAILABLE(10_10, 8_0)`), and the gate raised nothing on their files; the rows of `features8.json` are the header's.
- **The `CIFeatureType*` values** are the strings the host's constants hold (`QRCode`, `Rectangle`, `Text`), printed by the
  differential's constants line.
